Lab

Plant one real rule in a pile of noise.

Everything here runs in your browser. The lab generates noise columns plus one planted link, source=a → target=yes, mines every two-item rule, then mines shuffled copies to build the ghost distribution. A rule is called real only if it beats the strongest ghost often enough.

The right answer is nothing

Rules are still mined, often dozens. The validator should reject all of them. Occasional single survivors are the 5% error rate the test allows.

Ghosts get stronger

Watch the strongest ghost move right. A fixed lift cut-off that worked on one dataset fails on the next; a null-calibrated bar moves with the data.

Null runs < 19

An inert test

With R runs the smallest p-value is 1/(R+1). Below 19 runs nothing can reach 0.05, and a real effect is reported as absent. The Python package refuses this.

This lab is simplified: two-item rules, one null model, no redundancy filtering. The full method adds those.
